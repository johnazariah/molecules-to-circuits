Chapter 16: The CNOT Staircase

Centre for Quantum Software & Information, University of Technology Sydney

Every Pauli rotation becomes a sequence of elementary gates. This chapter shows exactly how — and connects the dots from encoding choice to circuit cost.

In This Chapter¶

  • What you’ll learn: How e−iθPe^{-i\theta P}e−iθP decomposes into basis-change gates, a CNOT chain, and an RzR_zRz​ rotation. Why the cost is exactly 2(w−1)2(w-1)2(w−1) CNOTs. How to trace a complete decomposition for the XXYYXXYYXXYY exchange term.

  • Why this matters: This is where everything converges. Encoding choice (Chapter 7), tapering (Chapters 10–13), and Trotterization (Chapters 14–15) all reduce to one question: how many times do we run this staircase, and how tall is it?

  • Prerequisites: Chapters 4 (gates), 14–15 (Trotter decomposition).

The Big Picture¶

Recall from Chapter 4 the CNOT staircase formula: a Pauli rotation with weight www costs 2(w−1)2(w-1)2(w−1) CNOT gates. We stated this without proof. Now let’s see why — by building the decomposition step by step.

The intuition: a weight-www Pauli rotation e−iθPe^{-i\theta P}e−iθP applies a phase rotation that depends on the collective parity of www qubits. No single qubit carries enough information — we need to temporarily entangle the www qubits (using CNOTs) so that their joint parity lives on one qubit, apply the rotation there, and then disentangle. The “staircase” is the entangling chain.

The Decomposition Recipe¶

A Pauli rotation e−iθPe^{-i\theta P}e−iθP for a weight-www Pauli string PPP is implemented in three phases:

Phase 1: Basis change¶

For each qubit position in PPP:

  • If Pj=XP_j = XPj​=X: apply Hadamard (HHH) to convert X-basis to Z-basis

  • If Pj=YP_j = YPj​=Y: apply Rx(π/2)R_x(\pi/2)Rx​(π/2) to convert Y-basis to Z-basis

  • If Pj=ZP_j = ZPj​=Z or III: no gate needed

After this step, all non-identity Pauli operators have been converted to ZZZ.

Phase 2: CNOT staircase¶

Chain CNOTs between the www non-identity qubits:

This creates a parity computation: qubit qw−1q_{w-1}qw−1​ now holds the XOR of all www qubits’ Z-values.

Phase 3: Rz rotation¶

Apply Rz(2θ)R_z(2\theta)Rz​(2θ) to the last qubit in the chain. This imprints the rotation angle, conditioned on the collective parity.

Phases 4–5: Uncompute¶

Reverse the CNOT staircase (same gates, reversed order), then reverse the basis-change gates. The total circuit is self-inverse up to the rotation.

Key insight: CNOTs scale linearly with weight. This is why encoding choice matters — reducing the maximum Pauli weight from 100 (JW) to 5 (ternary tree) saves 190 CNOTs per rotation.

Worked Example: ZZ Rotation (The Cheap Case)¶

Decompose e−iθ Z0Z1e^{-i\theta\, Z_0 Z_1}e−iθZ0​Z1​:

Basis change: Both qubits are already Z — no gates needed.

CNOT staircase: CNOT(0→1). One gate.

Rz: Rz(2θ)R_z(2\theta)Rz​(2θ) on qubit 1.

Reverse staircase: CNOT(0→1). One gate.

Total: 2 CNOTs + 1 single-qubit gate. The entire rotation is five gates — fast, shallow, cheap. This is the Coulomb repulsion term: the “classical” part of the Hamiltonian.

Worked Example: XXYY Rotation (The Expensive Case)¶

Decompose e−iθ X0X1Y2Y3e^{-i\theta\, X_0 X_1 Y_2 Y_3}e−iθX0​X1​Y2​Y3​:

Basis change:

  • Qubit 0: X→ZX \to ZX→Z via Hadamard

  • Qubit 1: X→ZX \to ZX→Z via Hadamard

  • Qubit 2: Y→ZY \to ZY→Z via Rx(π/2)R_x(\pi/2)Rx​(π/2)

  • Qubit 3: Y→ZY \to ZY→Z via Rx(π/2)R_x(\pi/2)Rx​(π/2)

After basis change: the operation is now e−iθ Z0Z1Z2Z3e^{-i\theta\, Z_0 Z_1 Z_2 Z_3}e−iθZ0​Z1​Z2​Z3​

CNOT staircase: CNOT(0→1), CNOT(1→2), CNOT(2→3)

Rz: Rz(2θ)R_z(2\theta)Rz​(2θ) on qubit 3

Reverse CNOT staircase: CNOT(2→3), CNOT(1→2), CNOT(0→1)

Undo basis change: Rx(−π/2)R_x(-\pi/2)Rx​(−π/2) on qubits 2,3; Hadamard on qubits 0,1

Total: 6 CNOTs + 9 single-qubit gates.

Key Takeaways¶

  • Each Pauli rotation decomposes into basis change → CNOT staircase → RzR_zRz​ → reverse.

  • The CNOT count is exactly 2(w−1)2(w-1)2(w−1) per rotation, where www is the Pauli weight.

  • Total CNOTs per Trotter step: ∑k2(wk−1)\sum_k 2(w_k - 1)∑k​2(wk​−1) — the single most important number for circuit feasibility.

  • Encoding choice and tapering both reduce wkw_kwk​ and therefore CNOT cost.
